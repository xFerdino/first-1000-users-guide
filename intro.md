## How to use this guide

This is a list of places where an indie developer in the US or Europe can put a product in front of its first users. It was inspired by [1000UserGuide](https://github.com/naxiaoduo/1000UserGuide), a Chinese list of 300+ channels. That list is mostly about the Chinese market, and its own README warns that many links have died. This one keeps the idea and rebuilds the list for Western channels, with every link checked.

**Left out on purpose:** the Chinese-market sections, and the generic "submit your URL" web directories (Free PR Web Directory and the like). Those bring backlinks of doubtful value and almost no users.

### Reading an entry

Every entry has the same parts:

- **Name and link.** If submitting happens on a different page, that link is given too.
- **Cost:** free, paid, or freemium. Freemium usually means a free queue that takes weeks, or a free listing that needs a backlink to the site.
- **What it is**, in one sentence.
- **The rule that matters:** what gets your post removed, what has to be true of your product, and which thread or day self-promotion belongs on.
- **Where that rule comes from:**
  - ● means the rule was read on the channel's own pages on the check date.
  - ◐ means it was read through a mirror site, because Reddit blocks automated readers.
  - ○ means it is general knowledge that wasn't checked on the site. Read the channel's own rules before you post.
- **Status:** a ⚠ marks a site that is alive but blocked our checker (bot walls, Reddit). Open it yourself before relying on it.

Links were checked on the date at the top of the page. Any link checked as dead was either fixed or removed.

### Five rules that apply everywhere

1. **Pick by audience, not by count.** Ten posts where your users already gather beat a hundred directory submissions. Start with the communities your users visit when they have the problem you solve, which are rarely launch sites.
2. **Read the rules before the first post.** Most removals and bans come from posting in the wrong thread or on the wrong day. Many communities now push all self-promotion into a weekly thread or a sister community.
3. **Give before you ask.** Answer questions and comment on other people's work for a few weeks first. An account with no history that turns up with a link reads as spam, and gets treated that way.
4. **Say that you made it.** Disclose it in every post and every answer that mentions your product. Stack Exchange requires it, Reddit moderators expect it, and readers forgive a pitch far more easily than a hidden one.
5. **Tag every link so you can tell what worked.** Add `?utm_source=<channel>` to web links. For a Google Play link, add `&referrer=utm_source%3D<channel>`; Play Console then reports installs by source. After a month, drop the channels that brought nothing.

### The order that usually works

1. **Quiet feedback first:** beta-tester communities, your own chat group, a handful of honest users.
2. **Listings next:** directories and "alternative to" sites. They are slow, but they bring search traffic for years.
3. **Niche communities:** places where your specific users meet. Most of your first thousand users come from here.
4. **One-shot launches last:** Show HN and Product Hunt. You get one good attempt at each, so make it when the product is solid and you have time to answer every comment that day.
